Our Story

About First Baptist

Who we are, what we believe, and how this congregation came to be in the high desert of Tehachapi.

Whether you've walked through our doors before or this is your first time even reading our name, we're glad you're here. First Baptist Tehachapi is a congregation given to the plain teaching of Scripture, the ordinary means of grace, and the slow, faithful work of following Christ together in the high desert.

We don't dress up the gospel or dress down the truth. We gather to sing, to pray, to hear God's Word opened verse by verse, and to remember that the God who sustains Israel in the wilderness sustains His people still.

What We Believe

Rooted in the historic faith

Our statement of faith, tap any article to read in full.

I. The Scriptures

The Holy Bible was written by men divinely inspired and is God's revelation of Himself to man. It is a perfect treasure of divine instruction. It has God for its author, salvation for its end, and truth, without any mixture of error, for its matter. All Scripture is a testimony to Christ, who is Himself the focus of divine revelation.

II. God

There is one and only one living and true God, an intelligent, spiritual, and personal Being, infinite in holiness and all other perfections. The eternal triune God reveals Himself to us as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, with distinct personal attributes, but without division of nature, essence, or being.

God the Father reigns with providential care over His universe and His creatures according to the purposes of His grace.

God the Son is the eternal Son of God, conceived of the Holy Spirit and born of the virgin Mary. He honored the divine law by His personal obedience, and in His substitutionary death on the cross He made provision for the redemption of men from sin. He was raised bodily, ascended into heaven, and is now exalted at the right hand of God. He will return in power and glory to judge the world.

God the Holy Spirit inspired the Scriptures, convicts men of sin, calls them to the Savior, and effects regeneration. He baptizes every believer into the Body of Christ, cultivates Christian character, and bestows spiritual gifts.

III. Man

Man is the special creation of God, made in His own image, male and female. By his free choice man sinned against God and brought sin into the human race; his posterity inherit a nature inclined toward sin. Only the grace of God can bring man into holy fellowship with Him. Every person of every race possesses full dignity and is worthy of respect and Christian love.

IV. Salvation

Salvation involves the redemption of the whole man and is offered freely to all who accept Jesus Christ as Lord and Savior. There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ.

Regeneration is a work of God's grace whereby believers become new creatures in Christ, through repentance toward God and faith in the Lord Jesus.

Justification is God's gracious and full acquittal of all who repent and believe in Christ, bringing peace and favor with God.

Sanctification is the ongoing experience by which the believer is set apart to God's purposes and enabled to progress toward spiritual maturity.

Glorification is the culmination of salvation, the final blessed state of the redeemed.

V. God's Purpose of Grace

Election is the gracious purpose of God by which He regenerates, justifies, sanctifies, and glorifies sinners — the glorious display of God's sovereign goodness. All true believers endure to the end; those God has accepted in Christ and sanctified by His Spirit will never fall away from grace, but shall persevere and be kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation.

VI. The Church

A New Testament church is an autonomous local congregation of baptized believers, associated by covenant in the faith and fellowship of the gospel, observing the two ordinances of Christ and governed by His laws. Its scriptural officers are pastors and deacons; while both men and women are gifted for service in the church, the office of pastor is limited to men as qualified by Scripture.

VII. Baptism and the Lord's Supper

Christian baptism is the immersion of a believer in water in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — an act of obedience symbolizing the believer's faith in a crucified, buried, and risen Savior. It is prerequisite to the privileges of church membership and the Lord's Supper.

The Lord's Supper is a symbolic act of obedience whereby members, through partaking of the bread and the fruit of the vine, memorialize the death of the Redeemer and anticipate His second coming.

VIII. The Lord's Day

The first day of the week is the Lord's Day, a Christian institution for regular observance, commemorating the resurrection of Christ and including exercises of worship and spiritual devotion, both public and private.

IX. The Kingdom

The Kingdom of God includes both His general sovereignty over the universe and His particular kingship over men who acknowledge Him as King. Christians ought to pray and labor that the Kingdom may come and God's will be done on earth, awaiting the full consummation at Christ's return.

X. Last Things

God will bring the world to its appropriate end. Jesus Christ will return personally and visibly in glory; the dead will be raised, and Christ will judge all men in righteousness. The unrighteous will be consigned to Hell; the righteous, in resurrected and glorified bodies, will dwell forever in Heaven with the Lord.

XI. Evangelism and Missions

It is the duty and privilege of every follower of Christ to make disciples of all nations. Missionary effort rests upon a spiritual necessity of the regenerate life and is expressly commanded by Christ — every believer is called to win the lost by verbal witness undergirded by a Christian lifestyle.

XII. Education

Christianity is the faith of enlightenment and intelligence. The new birth opens the human faculties and creates a thirst for knowledge; Christian education is necessary to a complete spiritual program, always under the pre-eminence of Jesus Christ and the authority of Scripture.

XIII. Stewardship

God is the source of all blessings, temporal and spiritual. Christians are under obligation to serve Him with time, talents, and possessions, and should contribute cheerfully, regularly, and proportionately for the advancement of the Redeemer's cause on earth.

XIV. Cooperation

Christ's people should organize associations and conventions to secure cooperation for the objects of the Kingdom of God. Such bodies are voluntary and advisory, with no authority over the churches, designed to combine and direct energies for common gospel ends.

XV. The Christian and the Social Order

All Christians are under obligation to seek Christ's will in their own lives and in society. In the spirit of Christ, Christians should oppose racism and every form of greed, selfishness, and vice, and work to provide for the orphaned, needy, abused, aged, helpless, and sick, speaking on behalf of the unborn and the sanctity of human life from conception to natural death.

XVI. Peace and War

It is the duty of Christians to seek peace with all men on principles of righteousness, and to do all in their power, in accordance with the spirit of Christ, to put an end to war — praying for the reign of the Prince of Peace.

XVII. Religious Liberty

God alone is Lord of the conscience. Church and state should be separate; the state owes every church protection and full freedom in pursuit of its spiritual ends, without favoring any one group. A free church in a free state is the Christian ideal.

XVIII. The Family

God has ordained the family as the foundational institution of human society. Marriage is the uniting of one man and one woman in covenant commitment for a lifetime. Husband and wife are of equal worth before God; a husband is to love his wife as Christ loved the church, and a wife is to respect her husband and serve as his helper. Children, from the moment of conception, are a blessing and heritage from the Lord, to be taught spiritual and moral values and led toward choices based on biblical truth.
