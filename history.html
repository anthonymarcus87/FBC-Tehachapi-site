Since 1942

Our History

Eight decades of worship, service, and faithfulness in the high desert of Tehachapi.

First Baptist Tehachapi began in 1942 as a direct answer to prayer. Mrs. Lee T. Holland, a Northern Baptist woman suffering from cancer, longed to see a Southern Baptist church established in her adopted mountain town before she died. In the spring of 1942, six women began meeting weekly, inspired by her faith, to organize a Woman's Missionary Society. That November, with $22.60 raised between them, they wrote to the Home Mission Board for help and were told there were no funds available — so they moved forward anyway, renting meeting space under the Juanita Hotel and reaching out to the Southern Baptist Convention.

The church was formally chartered on December 17, 1942, with twelve charter members. Brother F.E. "Dick" Loving became the first pastor, paid $10 a week. The young congregation met first in the Odd Fellows Hall (later the Santa Fe Hotel), then in 1946 built its first building at 414 S. Curry Street — constructed partly from lumber and materials taken from the Manzanar internment camp near Lone Pine, where thousands of Japanese Americans had been unjustly incarcerated during the war.

The congregation grew steadily through the 1950s and 60s under a succession of pastors, purchasing property at 1049 S. Curry Street in 1955 — the same address the church calls home today. A new sanctuary was completed there in 1970, with further building projects following in 1982 and 1984. Along the way the church helped establish the Mojave Church as a mission point (1947) and later the Twin Oaks Mission (1978), reflecting a consistent heart for planting and supporting other congregations.

By its 50th anniversary in 1992, under Pastor Jerry McCullah, the church had adopted a new constitution, launched a Spanish-language mission led by Ruben Viveros, and was running an active food-share ministry alongside Men's and Women's fellowship groups — the same rhythm of worship, service, and mission that continues today.

The 25 years that followed brought a steady rotation of pastors — Jerry McCullah continued through 1999, followed by Larry Wood, Mark Vaughan, and Ron Barker, with Don Ledbetter serving as interim before John M. Lopez, Th.D was welcomed as pastor in March 2017, his wife Nita serving alongside him as Music Director. That same October, the church marked its 75th anniversary with former pastor Jerry McCullah returning as guest speaker — a fitting way to celebrate 75 years in a town that, in 1942, didn't yet have a single Southern Baptist church to call home. Pastor Lopez served until 2025, and in 2026 the church welcomed Marcus Lewis as its current Lead Pastor.

A Living Link to 1942

As of the 75th anniversary in 2017, charter member Everett Sims was still an active, faithful member of the church — at age 102, still attending services regularly. He and his wife Adalee moved to Tehachapi from Texas in 1940 and helped start the church two years later. Over more than seven decades he served as deacon, Sunday School teacher, trustee, and choir member, and put his own carpentry and handyman skills to work on the church's buildings whenever something needed doing. He turned 103 that November.

A Few Things You Might Not Know

  • The first pastor was paid just $10 a week.
  • In 1942, the church couldn't rent a house in Tehachapi because the town had only a Catholic church, a community church, and a Four Square Gospel church — landlords assumed a pastor would need a mechanic's job to make ends meet.
  • In 1945, the church met above a bar and had to clean up before Sunday morning services.
  • During World War II, the church displayed a silk flag bordered in red with a blue star placed on the white field for each family member serving — 35 stars in all, with the congregation gathering to pray for each one by name every Sunday.
  • The first building (1946) was built using materials from the Manzanar internment camp near Lone Pine.
  • A 1992 revival meeting saw the church swimming pool used for baptisms — the associate pastor's grandson, Eddie Parks, was baptized in Patty and Grant Snyder's outdoor pool while it was snowing.
  • The original property at 414 S. Curry Street was sold in the 1960s, and the lumber from the very first building was reused again — carried onto the property at 1049 S. Curry as a gift toward the new construction.
  • The first service in the present sanctuary was held February 21, 1982, with a debt-free dedication just two weeks later on March 7, 1982.
  • Of the original 35 blue stars sewn onto the WWII prayer flag — one for each church family member serving — not one ever had to be turned to gold.
  • As of the church's 75th anniversary in 2017, charter member Everett Sims was still an active member at age 102, the last living link to the founding congregation.
